For a draft you have written

Writing partner

Ten skills for a draft you have already written. Each one reviews as a supervisor would: it identifies the problem, explains why it matters, points to the exact place, and hands the revision back to you. None of them drafts, rewrites, or supplies replacement prose. Start with the gate, then either run the full review or pick the check you need.

Something to practise on

Practice drafts

Five fictional student drafts, so you can watch a review skill work before you hand it your own writing. Each one has real problems in it — that is the point. Download a draft, install a skill, upload the file, and ask for the review.

Everything in these files is invented. The parties, the facts, the drafting, and the student authors are fictional, and the documents were written as teaching samples. Do not rely on any authority cited in them, and do not treat any of them as a model answer.

A one-page brief of a negligence appeal: facts, procedural history, issue, rule, holding, reasoning, takeaway. The rule statement claims more than the case decides.

What every skill has in common

Shared principles

The skills take a problem-first approach and build in the PAUSE Rule, Stanford Law School student AI guidance, Responsible AI at Stanford, productive struggle, legal-source verification, structured and manageable data, transparent AI-use logs, and accurate, non-anthropomorphic explanations of how AI systems work.
